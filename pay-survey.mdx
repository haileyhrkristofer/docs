## What is a pay survey?

A pay survey in Hailey helps you analyze salaries and benefits between women and men, both within equal work and equivalent work. The feature provides a clear overview of the data, making it easier to identify pay differences, document deviations and continue working with the analysis.

## Before you get started

Before you initiate the pay survey, you need to review the data and make sure that all information to be included is correctly filled in. This is important because the pay survey is only based on the information available when the survey is started.

Information added or updated after the pay survey has been started will not be shown in the pay survey. For an employee to be included in the analysis, the following information needs to be in place:

- Title/Titles
- Salary/Salaries (approved)
- Any benefits (approved)
- Date of birth
- Total employment time
- Legal entity
- Gender (Female or Male)

## Initiate pay survey

Once you are satisfied with the data, it is time to initiate the pay survey. Go to **HR Hub - Pay surveys - Initiate.** A wizard will then open and guide you step by step through the setup.

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-29-48-4734-PM.png?width=670&height=491&name=image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-29-48-4734-PM.png)

Begin by entering a name for the pay survey. Then select the legal entity or entities to include. Employees belonging to the selected legal entities will be included in the pay survey. If there are no legal entities in the account, all employees will be included.

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-29-04-8205-PM.png?width=670&height=419&name=image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-29-04-8205-PM.png)

Continue by selecting a date for evaluation. The selected date determines which employee data will be included in the pay survey.

<Warning>
  The pay survey is based on the salaries and organizational affiliation that apply on the selected date. Changes made after this date are not included.
</Warning>

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-33-10-0510-PM.png?width=670&height=421&name=image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-33-10-0510-PM.png)

Next, choose whether the amounts should be calculated as monthly or yearly amounts. This choice determines how the amounts are converted in the pay survey, so that they can be compared consistently.

If you want to include hourly salaries, turn on **Include hourly salaries**. Then enter the company’s full-time equivalent weekly hours in **Full-time equivalent weekly hours**, for example 40. This information is used to convert hourly salaries to a monthly or yearly amount. If hourly salaries should not be included, leave **Include hourly salaries** turned off.

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-34-21-6616-PM.png?width=670&height=419&name=image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-34-21-6616-PM.png)

In the next step, choose which variable pay types should be included in the pay survey. Active variable pay types are included by default. Deselect any you want to leave out of this survey.

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Sep-24-2026-11-28-45-1228-AM.png?width=670&height=388&name=image-png-Sep-24-2026-11-28-45-1228-AM.png)

In the next step, choose which benefits should be included in the pay survey. By default, all benefits that apply to the selected legal entities are selected, but you can deselect the benefits that should not be included. If there are no benefits for the selected legal entities, this step is skipped.

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-35-06-3381-PM.png?width=670&height=422&name=image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-35-06-3381-PM.png)

## Start

Once the pay survey has been initiated, the **Start** step opens. Here, you get an overview of the pay survey’s basic details, such as which legal entities, employees and benefits are included, which date was used for the snapshot, and when the pay survey was created.

Under **Facts**, you also see a summary of the compensation data, such as the Female/Male distribution, average and median compensation, and the gender pay gap. Amounts are shown based on the calculation method selected when the pay survey was initiated, either monthly or yearly.

Under **Implications of your pay gap**, you can also see examples of what the current pay gap represents, such as the time during the workday when women stop being paid, the number of extra days per year women would need to work to match men's pay, and that year's Equal Pay Day.

On the right, you will find a brief overview of the steps included in the pay survey. Use this view to get an initial picture of the data before moving on to **Preparation.**

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-25-29-9619-PM.png?width=670&height=325&name=image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-25-29-9619-PM.png)

## Preparation

Once the pay survey has been initiated, it is time to prepare it. This is done by reviewing and updating the survey parameters. By default, the pay survey has four parameters, each weighted equally at 25%.

Click Update parameters to:

- **Add another parameter**
- **Rename existing parameters**
- **Delete parameters**
- **Edit the weight in percentage**

If needed, you can group two or more titles into a title group, for example if you have similar roles that you want to analyze together. You create a title group by clicking **Manage titles - Add title group.**

Finish the preparation by scoring each parameter for each title or title group. The scale is 0 to 5. When you are done, click **Save preparations** before moving on to the next step.

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-07-2026-12-31-26-1553-PM.png?width=670&height=342&name=image-png-Jul-07-2026-12-31-26-1553-PM.png)

If employees have salaries in different currencies, the exchange rates are retrieved from the company settings. If needed, you can adjust the exchange rates directly in the pay survey.

Click **the three dots** - **Update exchange rates**. Then select the base currency and enter the correct exchange rate. Finish by clicking **Save**.

Read more about how to set up currencies [here](/admin-get-started#general-currencies).

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-07-2026-12-35-17-9745-PM.png?width=670&height=300&name=image-png-Jul-07-2026-12-35-17-9745-PM.png)

Analysis

Once the pay survey has been prepared, it is time to start the analysis. You can analyze the data in two ways: **equal work** and **equivalent work.** Equal work is based on titles and title groups, while equivalent work is based on the scoring completed during Preparation. This means that you can compare work with the same or similar scores, even if the titles are different.

In the analysis, the amounts are shown based on the choice you made during initiation, meaning monthly or yearly. If hourly salaries were included, they are converted and included in the salary data. If benefits were included, they are shown as a separate option in the analysis view and can be displayed separately or together with salaries.

Under the **Equal work** tab, the analysis is based on titles and the title groups you created in the previous steps. Here, you can see the number of Female/Male employees, the percentage of Female employees and the gender pay gap for each title/group.

For a more detailed analysis, click a title/title group. This opens a view that shows the number of Female and Male employees, percentage of Female employees, average age, average salary, median salary and gender pay gap in percent.

In the analysis view, you can choose which parts of the data should be included in the calculations. You can select salaries, variable pay, benefits, or all three. This affects the values shown in the analysis, such as averages, medians, graphs, and charts.

You will also find a graph and a chart in this view. The graph shows salaries by gender in relation to total employment time or age, and the chart is a box chart showing salaries by gender.

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-26-12-3820-PM.png?width=670&height=604&name=image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-26-12-3820-PM.png)

Under the **Equivalent work** tab, the analysis is shown in a similar way to **Equal work**. Here, you can see the number of Female and Male employees, the percentage of Female employees and the gender pay gap for each title or title group.

A score is also shown, based on the assessment of the parameters in the previous step. The score helps you identify titles and title groups that can be considered equivalent.

For a more detailed analysis, select two or more titles or title groups and click **Start analysis**. This opens the same analysis view as for equal work.

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-26-37-0022-PM.png?width=670&height=557&name=image-png-Jul-06-2026-12-26-37-0022-PM.png)

<Note>
  - A positive gender pay gap, for example 15%, means a gap to the detriment of **Female** employees.
  - A negative gender pay gap, for example -15%, means a gap to the detriment of **Male** employees.
</Note>

## Identify & handle deviations

During the analysis, you can add deviations if needed. Click **Add deviation** in the analysis view. A window will then open where you can describe the deviation and classify it as **Justified**, **Unjustified** or **In need of investigation**.

When you are done, click **Save**. Saved deviations are shown under **Documentation** - **Deviations**. You can also edit a deviation by clicking it.

<Note>
  To finalize the pay survey, all deviations need to be classified as **Justified** or **Unjustified**. Deviations marked as **In need of investigation** need to be updated before the pay survey can be finalized.
</Note>

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Nov-20-2024-04-05-02-3491-PM.png?width=670&height=475&name=image-png-Nov-20-2024-04-05-02-3491-PM.png)

## Finalize

When the analysis is complete and all deviations have been classified, go to **Documentation** - **Closing comments**. Here, you can add additional documentation beyond what has been added for each deviation.

Then click **Finalize pay survey** in the top-right corner. Once the pay survey has been finalized, it can no longer be edited.

![](https://docs.haileyhr.com/hs-fs/hubfs/image-png-Jul-07-2026-12-36-49-2602-PM.png?width=670&height=300&name=image-png-Jul-07-2026-12-36-49-2602-PM.png)

Ongoing and finalized pay surveys are shown under **HR Hub** - **Pay surveys** and sorted by date.

## Key terms

Below you will find short explanations of terms that are useful to know when working with pay surveys.

- **Date for evaluation:** The date that determines which data the pay survey is based on. Salaries and organizational affiliation are based on how they appeared on the selected date.
- **Title group:** A collection of titles that are analyzed together in the pay survey.
- **Parameters:** Criteria used to score titles and title groups.
- **Equal work:** Work analyzed together based on title or title group.
- **Equivalent work:** Work compared based on the scoring completed during Preparation.
- **Deviation:** An identified pay difference that needs to be described and classified in the analysis.
- **Justified:** A classification used when there is an objective and documented explanation for the deviation.
- **Unjustified:** A classification used when there is no objective and documented explanation for the deviation.
- **In need of investigation:** A classification used when a deviation needs to be investigated further before it can be assessed as justified or unjustified.

## Rights

Admins have access to manage pay surveys by default. For other roles, the **Manage pay surveys** right needs to be activated.

Read more about how to set up roles and rights **[HERE](/roles-and-rights).**

- **Manage pay surveys** makes it possible to manage pay surveys in the account.

<Warning>
  Please note that individuals with the right to **Manage pay surveys** will have access to all information in the surveys, regardless of their other access rights, such as those related to salaries.
</Warning>

## Common questions

Here you’ll find frequently asked questions related to pay surveys.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How are employees registered as **Non-binary** or **Other** in the **Gender** field handled?">
    In this version of the pay survey, only employees registered as **Female** or **Male** in the **Gender** field are included. Employees registered as **Non-binary** or **Other** are therefore not included in the current analysis.

    This is because the analysis currently uses the legal gender categories in Sweden. Our goal is to develop a more inclusive analysis that supports fair and equal pay for all employees, regardless of gender identity. If you have ideas or suggestions on how this can be developed, please feel free to contact us.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which parameters should we use in the pay survey?">
    This is entirely up to you, based on how you want to evaluate roles in your organization. The parameters in the system are only suggestions and can be adjusted to fit your needs. You can add your own parameters, rename existing parameters, delete parameters and edit the weight in percentage.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can we change the score scale, for example to 1 to 10?">
    No, it is not possible to change the score scale at the moment. Scoring is done on a scale from 0 to 5.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I am creating a pay survey for 2023. Why is an employee not included?">
    For an employee to be included in the pay survey, the employment needs to have been active on the selected Date for evaluation. Therefore, check that the employee had an active employment on the date selected when the pay survey was initiated.

    Also check that all required data was in place when the pay survey was initiated, such as title, approved salary, date of birth, total employment time, legal entity and gender registered as Female or Male. Any data added or updated after the pay survey was initiated will not be reflected in the pay survey.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="An employee has been employed for three years. Why is only one year of total employment time shown?">
    The total employment time in the pay survey shows how long the employee has been employed within the legal entity they belong to on the date for evaluation.

    If the employee has previously belonged to other legal entities, that employment time is not included in the current calculation.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
